## D1 — Normalization & preprocessing
**Script:** `analyze_entropy.R`
**Decision:** Raw Visium spatial transcriptomics data are loaded from `data/<sample_name>/`. Spots are explicitly subsetted to in-tissue spots (`in_tissue == 1`) using `spatial/tissue_positions.csv` *before* applying depth quality control. Spots are then filtered using minimum thresholds of $nCount \ge 500$ and $nFeature \ge 250$, followed by coordinate alignment verification via `validate_imaged_coordinates()`. Gene expression filtering is computed *after* spot subsetting, retaining genes detected in at least $\max(20, \lceil 0.02 \times N_{\text{valid\_spots}} \rceil)$ spots. Normalization is performed directly via `SpaNorm::SpaNorm()` using negative binomial modeling (`gene.model = "nb"`), `adj.method = "logpac"`, `df.tps = 6`, `lambda.a = 1e-04`, a fixed random seed (`set.seed(23)`), and default sampling fraction (`sample.p = 0.25`). An S4 method for `SpaNorm::SpaNorm` is registered on `Seurat` objects using public Bioconductor APIs (`SpatialExperiment` and `SummarizedExperiment`), eliminating private `:::` function calls. QC metrics with explicit denominators (`Raw_Spots_Grid`, `Spots_On_Tissue`, `Spots_Post_Depth_QC`, `Spots_Final`, `Pct_OnTissue_Retained`, `Raw_Genes`, `Genes_Detected`, `Genes_Post_Filter`, `Pct_Genes_Retained_OfAll`, `Pct_Genes_Retained_OfDetected`, `Mean_Percent_MT`, `Mean_Percent_Ribo`) are logged per sample to `results/analyze_entropy/<sample_name>_qc_metrics.csv`.
**Rationale:** `Load10X_Spatial(filter.matrix = TRUE)` loads all 14,336 grid barcodes when reading raw feature matrices. Explicitly subsetting on `in_tissue == 1` before depth QC ensures that QC denominators reflect true tissue area rather than the unutilised slide background (2,623 off-tissue barcodes in sample1). Computing gene filtering thresholds after spot subsetting prevents dropped low-quality spots from falsely inflating gene retention. Gene retention is reported against two denominators because the probe panel emits the full GRCh38 feature list: `Pct_Genes_Retained_OfAll` uses all 37,082 rows, while `Pct_Genes_Retained_OfDetected` uses only rows with at least one count, excluding the ~18,100 features that are structurally zero because no probe targets them (Stage 0). The second figure is the biologically meaningful one; in sample1 the two differ by roughly a factor of two. SpaNorm removes technical spatial noise and sequencing depth confounders while preserving spatial tissue architecture.
**Alternatives considered / rejected:** SCTransform and standard log-normalization were rejected because they do not explicitly model spatial coordinates or adjust for spatial technical autocorrelation. QC thresholds of $nCount \ge 500$ & $nFeature \ge 250$ retain 99.97% of on-tissue spots in sample1 (11,709 of 11,713, dropping 4 marginal spots) and 91.2%–99.97% across the 22-sample cohort, so the earlier claim that this cutoff preferentially removes necrotic centres is not supported for sample1. The four lowest-retention samples are also the four shallowest (sample20 91.2%, median $nCount$ 2,995; sample21 92.08%; sample2 93.35%, median 1,785; sample16 94.59%, median 2,498), where up to ~500 in-tissue spots are dropped. Whether those dropped spots are spatially clustered has **not** been checked, so no claim is made about which anatomical zones they belong to.
**Status:** implemented
**Review notes:** Replaced internal `:::` fitting architecture with direct `SpaNorm::SpaNorm()` method dispatch on `Seurat` objects. Pinned `bioconductor-spanorm=1.4.0=r45hdfd78af_0` and `bioconductor-spatialexperiment=1.20.0=r45hdfd78af_0` in `environment.yml`. Logged explicit on-tissue QC metrics per sample. `validate_imaged_coordinates()` is a no-op once the `in_tissue == 1` subset runs first, but its result is recorded as `Spots_Final` so the retention percentage cannot silently drift from the object actually analysed. Stage 0 note: the raw h5 barcode grid is 14,336 in 15 of 22 samples and 14,327–14,335 in the rest; `tissue_positions.csv` carries all 14,336 rows in every sample and the missing barcodes are all off-tissue, so no in-tissue spot is lost.

## D2 — Shannon entropy formulation
**Script:** `R/shannon_entropy.R` (called from `analyze_entropy.R`)
**Decision:** Shannon entropy is computed per spot as $H = -\sum_{i} p_i \log_2(p_i)$, where $p_i = y_i / \sum_j y_j$ represents the relative expression proportion of gene $i$ within that spot. Mitochondrial (`^MT-`) and ribosomal (`^RP[SL]`) gene families are filtered from the expression matrix prior to entropy calculation via `exclude_pattern = "^(MT-|RP[SL])"`. In `analyze_entropy.R`, entropy is computed twice: once on raw counts (`layer = "counts"`, stored as `shannon_entropy_raw`) before normalization, and once on normalized expression (`layer = "data"`, stored as `shannon_entropy`) after SpaNorm. Calculations are executed via sparse matrix operations (`dgCMatrix`) to optimize memory efficiency.
**Rationale:** Stage 0 cohort analysis established that all 103 `^RP[SL]` rows are present in the feature list but carry exactly 0 counts in all 22 samples (`n_RPSL_nonzero = 0`). The most likely explanation is that the Visium CytAssist Human Transcriptome probe set (Visium V5 Slide chemistry) contains no probes for cytoplasmic ribosomal protein genes, which would also explain the ~18,100 other structurally zero rows; this has not been verified against 10x probe-set documentation and is stated here as an inference, not as a confirmed design fact. What is measured is the zero count itself. Non-zero mitochondrial transcript content was measured at 2.46%–10.31% of total counts (mean spot MT ~3.4%–16.8% across samples). Excluding MT transcripts removes metabolic and hypoxia-driven artifacts, while `exclude_pattern = "^(MT-|RP[SL])"` acts in practice as an MT-only filter given zero ribosomal probe representation.
**Alternatives considered / rejected:** Including MT transcripts in entropy calculation was rejected because spot-level entropy would be distorted by variable mitochondrial transcription. Top highly variable genes (HVGs) only was rejected because transcriptional plasticity spans broader gene sets. Linear back-transformation alone ($2^x - 1$) was evaluated in Stage 2 as an alternative to rarefaction and rejected: it fixes the log-transform category error, but a substantial depth-driven support ceiling remains in the raw counts it would be applied on top of ($\rho = 0.736$ vs $nCount$, $\rho = 0.822$ vs $nFeature$ for the raw plug-in estimator), which no rescaling of the normalized matrix can recover.
**Status:** implemented
**Review notes:** Confirmed ribosomal probe absence across cohort chemistry (`n_RPSL_nonzero = 0`). Documented empirical mitochondrial fractions: 2.46%–10.31% of total on-tissue counts, mean per-spot 3.40%–16.82% (`results/cohort_qc/chemistry_check.csv`). Shannon entropy filtering excludes MT features to capture phenotypic biological diversity.
Stage 2 Diagnostic (`diagnose_entropy_scaling.R` on sample1, $N = 11{,}709$ spots) evaluated the separation between (1) the log-transform category error and (2) the depth-driven support ceiling. Outputs: `results/statistical_tests/sample1_entropy_scaling_diagnostic.csv` (correlations), `..._degeneracy.csv` (regression of $H$ on $\log_2 K$), `..._value_range.csv` (spread of the stored non-zero values), `..._support.csv` (spread of the two detected-gene supports).

**Two supports exist and they are not interchangeable.** `n_detected_counts` is the number of genes with a non-zero *raw count*; `n_detected_norm` is the number with a non-zero *SpaNorm `logpac`* value. SpaNorm's `qnbinom` step both imputes genes with zero raw counts and zeroes genes with non-zero counts, so the two differ by a mean of 434 genes per spot and have very different spreads: `n_detected_counts` 558–11,321 (median 6,532) against `n_detected_norm` 5,114–8,305 (median 6,478). Entropy computed on the `data` layer sums over `n_detected_norm`, so that is the support the log-scale degeneracy must be tested against; entropy computed on `counts` sums over `n_detected_counts`, which is what Stage 3 rarefaction acts on. Both columns are reported below because the same claim takes very different values against each.

- **Log-transform category error.** SpaNorm's `logpac` adjustment is $\log_2(\text{qnbinom}(\cdot) + 1)$ (`SpaNorm:::normaliseLogPAC`), which compresses a 777-fold linear range into stored non-zero values with median 1.585, 99th percentile 4.170 and maximum 9.604. That flattens $p_i$ towards $1/K$ and collapses entropy onto $\log_2 K$: against `n_detected_norm`, $r = 0.9978$ and $\rho = 0.9990$, and regressing $H$ on $\log_2 K$ gives slope $1.0086$, intercept $-0.2449$, $R^2 = 0.9973$ (mean ratio $H / \log_2 K = 0.989$). The current normalized metric is a relabelled gene count. Against `n_detected_counts` the same estimator correlates at only $r = 0.7337$ — that lower figure is not the degeneracy and must not be quoted as it.
- **Linear back-transformation ($2^x - 1$).** The exact inverse of `logpac`, not an approximation, and safe on the sparse `@x` slot because $x = 0 \mapsto 0$ leaves structural zeros untouched. It resolves the category error: $r$ against `n_detected_norm` drops from $0.9978$ to $0.8471$, against $nCount$ from $0.654$ to $0.491$, and against $nFeature$ from $0.734$ to $0.572$.
- **Depth-driven support ceiling.** The evidence for the ceiling is the **raw counts plug-in** row — $\rho = 0.736$ vs $nCount$, $\rho = 0.822$ vs $nFeature$, $\rho = 0.822$ vs `n_detected_counts` — measured directly on the counts Stage 3 rarefaction will act on. A spot at 3,000 counts observes fewer genes than a spot at 10,000 counts, capping plug-in entropy regardless of biological plasticity. The residual in the linear row ($\rho = 0.683$ vs $nCount$, $0.754$ vs $nFeature$) is *not* a clean measure of that ceiling: linear entropy tracks SpaNorm's imputed support more strongly ($r = 0.8471$, $\rho = 0.8613$ vs `n_detected_norm`) than it tracks raw depth, so it is partly inheriting the narrowed `n_detected_norm` support rather than the raw one. The gate to Stage 3 rarefied entropy stands on the raw plug-in row.

## D3 — Entropy–depth & covariate QC and stemness correlation tests
**Script:** `R/entropy_correlation.R` (called from `analyze_entropy.R` and `analyze_stemness.R`)
**Decision:** Both parametric Pearson correlation ($r$) and non-parametric Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$) are computed alongside two-sided $p$-values, log10 $p$-values, 95% confidence intervals, and spot sample size ($N$):
1. In `analyze_entropy.R`: evaluates correlations for both `shannon_entropy_raw` and `shannon_entropy` against sequencing depth (`nCount`, `nFeature`) and the metabolic covariate `percent.mt`, outputting results to `results/statistical_tests/<sample_name>_entropy_correlations.csv` and a 6-panel scatterplot figure. `percent.ribo` is **not** a target: Stage 0 established that all 103 `^RP[SL]` rows are identically zero in all 22 samples (D2), so the covariate has no variance and correlating against it produced only all-NA rows and empty plot panels. It can still be passed explicitly via the `targets` argument should a future chemistry populate those rows.
2. In `analyze_stemness.R`: evaluates biological correlation between normalized `shannon_entropy` and `Stemness_Score1`, outputting results to `results/stemness_analysis/<sample_name>_stemness_correlations.csv` and `_plot.png`.
All summary CSV outputs store exact unfloored double-precision floats for `Pearson_p` and `Spearman_p`. Both correlations also carry a log10 $p$-value computed via Student's $t$ cumulative distribution in log space, $\log_{10}(p) = \log_{10}(2) + \frac{\text{pt}(-|t|, \text{df}, \text{log.p = TRUE})}{\ln(10)}$, to eliminate saturation at `0.00e+00`: `Pearson_log10_p` uses the $t$ statistic returned by `cor.test`, and `Spearman_log10_p` reconstructs $t = \rho\sqrt{(N-2)/(1-\rho^2)}$ on $N-2$ degrees of freedom, which is the same approximation `cor.test(..., method = "spearman", exact = FALSE)` uses. Pearson 95% CIs are taken from `cor.test`; Spearman 95% CIs are Fisher $z$-transformed, $z = \text{atanh}(\rho)$ with the Bonett–Wright standard error $\text{SE} = \sqrt{1.06/(N-3)}$. Spot count $N$ is reported alongside the leading `Sample` column.
**Rationale:** Pearson correlation assesses linear relationships, whereas Spearman correlation captures monotonic non-linear dependencies without normality assumptions. Testing entropy against `percent.mt` confirms whether spatial entropy patterns are confounded by tissue degradation. Unfloored floating-point $p$-values and log10 $p$-values enable downstream meta-analysis and FDR corrections across the cohort without truncation artifacts. The Bonett–Wright standard error is used for the Spearman interval because the plain Pearson form $1/\sqrt{N-3}$ understates interval width for rank correlations; at $N = 11{,}709$ the two differ only in the third decimal (e.g. $\rho = 0.8700$ gives $[0.8654, 0.8744]$ against $[0.8655, 0.8743]$), but the corrected form is the defensible one to report.
**Alternatives considered / rejected:** Storing formatted character strings in CSVs was rejected because it saturated small $p$-values at `"0.00e+00"`, preventing quantitative ranking and meta-analytic pooling.
**Status:** implemented
**Review notes:** Refactored `calculate_entropy_correlations()` in `R/entropy_correlation.R` to output numeric double columns, `Pearson_log10_p` and `Spearman_log10_p`, Pearson and Fisher-z Spearman 95% CIs, and sample size $N$. Formatted strings are restricted to plot display annotations, where $p$-values below $10^{-300}$ are rendered as `10^<log10p>` rather than `0.00e+00`. Removed `percent.ribo` from the default target set. Log10 $p$-values were re-derived independently and reproduce the emitted values exactly for sample1 (raw vs `nCount` $-1577.68897$, raw vs `nFeature` $-3639.89176$, normalized vs `percent.mt` $-61.68253$, matching `cor.test`'s $2.0771513\times10^{-62}$).

## D4 — Stemness gene reference / scoring
**Script:** `analyze_stemness.R`
**Decision:** Spot-level stemness is quantified using Seurat's `AddModuleScore()` with a curated panel of meningioma and neural stemness markers (`PROM1`, `SOX2`, `POU5F1`, `NANOG`, `NES`, `CD44`, `MYC`). Prior to scoring, per-gene spot detection rates, mean expression counts, and detection levels are calculated and saved to `results/stemness_analysis/<sample_name>_stemness_marker_detection.csv`. Variable marker panels are accepted across samples due to Visium dropouts, and the exact subset of detected vs missing markers is logged in `results/stemness_analysis/<sample_name>_stemness_qc.csv`. Random seed 23 (`set.seed(23)`) is initialized before scoring to ensure deterministic control feature binning and sampling. Updated Seurat objects containing `Stemness_Score1` are saved back to `results/seurat_objects/<sample_name>_spatial_obj.rds` for downstream marker discovery (`find_entropy_markers.R`).
**Rationale:** Evaluating expression of established stem cell transcription factors and surface markers provides a validated baseline reference for stemness in meningioma. Pre-evaluating marker detection ensures that researchers can verify whether module scores reflect multi-gene programs or are predominantly driven by single robust markers. Persisting the resulting scores in the Seurat object prevents redundant re-computation in downstream pipeline steps.
**Alternatives considered / rejected:** Pluripotency transcription factors including Oct-4 (`POU5F1`) and `SOX2` are known to be present in non-stem tumor cells and normal meningeal cells, which limits their specificity for cleanly distinguishing stem from non-stem populations. Measured detection rates in sample1 reveal heterogeneous expression: robust ($\ge 50\%$ of spots) `CD44` (79.2%), `MYC` (73.9%), `NES` (59.7%); moderate (5–50%) `SOX2` (18.6%), `POU5F1` (14.2%), `PROM1` (11.7%); low (1–5%) `NANOG` (2.1%). These tiers are the `detection_level` labels emitted by `analyze_stemness.R` and describe detection frequency only, not marker reliability: 14.2% for `POU5F1` and 11.7% for `PROM1` are implausibly high for meningioma, and `POU5F1` is known to multi-map against the `POU5F1B` pseudogene, so both are pending the Stage 5 cross-hybridisation check before being read as stemness signal. Candidate alternative or supplementary markers for future panels include Frizzled 9 (`FZD9`), `GFAP`, Vimentin (`VIM`), SSEA-4, `CD44`, `CD73` (`NT5E`), and `CD105` (`ENG`).
**Status:** implemented
**Review notes:** Added per-gene detection evaluation (`_stemness_marker_detection.csv`), per-sample marker presence tracking (`_stemness_qc.csv`), deterministic control gene selection (`set.seed(23)`), and unified correlation reporting via `R/entropy_correlation.R`.
